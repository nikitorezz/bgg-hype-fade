# Technical findings and validation

This is the technical companion to the [write-up](../reports/writeup.md). It lists every finding with its estimate and uncertainty, explains how the results were validated, and states the caveats. All numbers are generated by the pipeline and stored in [`reports/results/key_numbers.json`](../reports/results/key_numbers.json); model tables are in [`reports/results/`](../reports/results/).

**Sample.** 6,927 new releases that entered BoardGameGeek's ranking between January 2017 and October 2023 and were still ranked 36 months later (the *main sample*); 6,918 are observed at months 12, 24 and 36 (the *balanced sample*). Each game counts equally unless stated otherwise. Intervals are 95%: standard errors across games for means, a game-level bootstrap for ratios.

---

## Findings

### F1. Launch ratings oversell, and the correction is front-loaded

The displayed average falls **0.40 points over 36 months** (95% CI 0.39–0.41): 0.24 by month 12 and 0.34 by month 24, so **61%** of the 3-year decline (CI 60–62%) happens in year one. **87%** of games end lower than they started; the median change is −0.38.

A control group of 2,428 established games (already ranked in October 2016 with at least 500 raters, present in every snapshot) drifted by −0.016 over the same calendar windows. The control-adjusted change is −0.385, so about 96% of the decline is specific to new releases. A game fixed-effects event study gives identical coefficients (β12 = −0.244, β36 = −0.401).

### F2. The decline isn't finished at three years

Backing out the average of only the ratings added in the previous three months, new raters score games **0.15 below the displayed average at month 3 and 0.23 below at month 36**. The sign holds under all nine combinations of the usability thresholds (gap between −0.13 and −0.26).

### F3. Hype does not predict how far a game falls

"Hype" is growth in raters over the first six months (log of raters at month 6 over raters at entry). Its raw correlation with the 3-year change is 0.01.

| Specification | Hype coefficient | SE |
|---|---|---|
| A. 3-year change, controlling for starting rating, size and launch year | +0.049 | 0.008 |
| B. Change after month 6 only, controlling for the month-6 rating | −0.004 | 0.013 |
| C. Reach (log raters at month 6) instead of growth | +0.049 | 0.008 |
| D. No controls besides launch year | +0.006 | 0.008 |

The hypothesis predicted a negative coefficient. None of the four specifications, and none of the robustness variants, gives a significantly negative one. With controls, more-hyped games fall slightly *less*; after the hype window, hype predicts nothing.

### F4. Hype predicts how *fast* a game falls

Mean change by month 6, from the least- to the most-hyped quartile: −0.10, −0.16, −0.21, −0.19. By month 36 the quartiles are close: −0.36, −0.40, −0.43, −0.41.

The mechanism is the running average. A game in the most-hyped quartile gains a median of 6× its entry raters within six months (least-hyped quartile: 1.25×), which dilutes the enthusiastic launch ratings quickly. For a game in the least-hyped quartile, the entry batch is still 46% of its raters at month 36, and new raters score it 0.30 below the displayed number. Hype changes the speed of the correction, not its size, and the slow movers haven't finished.

### F5. The starting rating predicts the size of the fall

Mean 3-year change by starting-rating decile runs from −0.05 (lowest decile, mean start 5.8) to −0.76 (highest decile, mean start 8.8); in Specification A the coefficient on the starting rating is −0.246. Part of this is regression to the mean, since launch averages rest on a median of 34 ratings; part is likely self-selection of the launch audience. The two can't be separated with this data.

### F6. Big games fade less

Weighted by raters at month 36, the mean change is −0.27. The 10% most-rated games lose 0.29, the rest 0.41.

### F7. Kickstarter games fade more (descriptive)

For 2017–2021 releases matched to a 2022 Kaggle metadata set (93–100% coverage), games flagged as Kickstarted fall 0.54 against 0.36. They start higher (7.62 against 7.23), but even within the same 0.5-wide starting-rating band they fall 0.06 to 0.16 more; the conditional gap in Specification A is −0.079 (CI −0.10 to −0.05), with no interaction with hype. The flag covers about 32% of matched games and is probably broader than "launched through crowdfunding".

### F8. A rating can be trusted after about 13 months

At entry, 32% of games are within ±0.25 of their 36-month rating. The first month at which at least 80% are within ±0.25 is **month 13** (bootstrap CI 13–14). Agreement at later months is partly mechanical, since the running average at month 24 already contains most of month 36's ratings.

An **adjusted launch rating** (OLS on the displayed rating, log raters and growth since entry; trained on 2017–2019 releases, tested on 2020–2023) is within ±0.25 for 55% of test games at entry (displayed rating: 31%), 77% at month 6 and 88% at month 12, and cuts the mean absolute error at entry from 0.48 to 0.29. It works mostly by shrinkage: the coefficient on the displayed rating at entry is 0.77.

### F9. The rank paradox

74% of games improve their BGG rank between entry and month 36 while their raw average falls. The rank is based on a Bayes average that pulls games with few ratings toward a low prior, so accumulating ratings lifts it.

## Validation

- **Data quality.** 36 automated checks pass ([`reports/qc/qc_report.md`](../reports/qc/qc_report.md)): complete monthly coverage, truncated files replaced, no duplicate (date, game) pairs, valid ranges, cohort counts matching an independent pilot, and ten spot-checked games matching exactly.
- **Sanity values.** 34 values computed independently before the analysis (sample sizes, curve points, the control drift, the gap, model coefficients, the trust curve) were reproduced within tolerance ([`reports/results/sanity_checks.csv`](../reports/results/sanity_checks.csv)).
- **Robustness.** Eight variants ([`reports/results/robustness.md`](../reports/results/robustness.md)): rater-weighted, excluding the 2020–2021 cohorts, popular vs other games, games entering with at least 50 raters, control-adjusted, and the unbalanced panel of all 11,020 new releases. The 36-month fade ranges from −0.27 to −0.41, 54–66% of it by month 12, and no variant gives a significantly negative hype coefficient.
- **Design choices.** Calendar-month fixed effects were not added to the event study because, with game effects and months-since-entry dummies, they are perfectly collinear (the age–period–cohort problem); the control group handles site-wide drift instead. Specification B measures the outcome after the hype window, which reduces mechanical regression to the mean. The adjusted-rating model is evaluated out of sample.

## Caveats

1. A game enters the ranking at 30 ratings, so its earliest ratings are folded into month 0. The analysis measures change after entry, not from the first rating.
2. Observational data: no causal claims. Rater growth also reflects print runs, distribution and price.
3. Regression to the mean and self-selection of the launch audience can't be separated cleanly (F5).
4. The new-rater average is approximate (two-decimal rounding, edited ratings) and is available for about 50–70% of game-months, more often for fast-growing games.
5. One enthusiast platform. The Kickstarter flag comes from a 2022 snapshot.
6. Monthly snapshots only; a daily-data robustness check was not run.
